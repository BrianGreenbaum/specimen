When **Google** commissioned a new house style for its internal design community in 2019, the brief landed at **Colophon Foundry**, the London studio known for work that is rigorous without being cold. The starting point was Poppins — Google Fonts’ most-downloaded geometric, all perfect circles and monoline charm — and the job was to make it *work harder*: tighter spacing, better economy, less decorative innocence.

The result, DM Sans (the initials honour DeepMind, its first big internal client), keeps the geometric skeleton and quietly disciplines it. Circles remain visible in the round letters, but apertures tighten, terminals square off slightly, and a tall x-height pulls everything toward text-size efficiency. Where Poppins smiles constantly, DM Sans smiles when spoken to.

Its second act came in 2023, when the single-weight release grew into this variable family: weight from 100 to an extravagant 1000, plus a genuine optical-size axis from 9 to 40. That opsz axis does real work — at caption sizes the letterforms loosen and open; at display sizes they sharpen and condense — making one file behave like a small type system.

Colophon’s fingerprints are subtle but findable: the slightly clipped curves, the no-nonsense italics, the way headlines stay composed rather than bouncy. It is geometric modernism edited by people who spend their days reading proofs.

For working designers DM Sans answers a very contemporary question: how do you give a product warmth and character while staying out of the user’s way? The Bauhaus asked how to rebuild the world from first principles. This is the same alphabet, a century on, asking something humbler — please be pleasant to read every day.
