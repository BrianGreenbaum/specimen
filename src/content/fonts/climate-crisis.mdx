In 2020 the Finnish newspaper **Helsingin Sanomat** wanted a way to make climate change visible in the one material a newspaper always has to hand: its own headlines. The result, designed by **Eino Korkala**, **Daniel Coull**, Eunyou Noh and Joohee Lee, is a typeface whose weight is not a style choice but a reading.

The heaviest cut stands for the extent of Arctic sea ice at its late-summer minimum in 1979. From there the axis follows the record kept by the US National Snow and Ice Data Center, year by year, to 2019, then carries on along a prediction to 2050.

The paper released it free, under an open licence, and since 2022 it has sat in Google Fonts next to the workhorses.
